Bertsekas is a pretty famous electrical engineer, kinda goated, seems to be really opinionated about the importance of control theory in finetuning and designing RL control loops for LLMs. 

More than a swift introduction to control theory, approximate dynamic programming (approximate because sometimes shit gets intractable), and bellman eqs and operators, this textbook for some reason puts *insane* emphasis that optimizations to the expected utility of policies are essentially 
> Newton steps on Bellman Operators

Which is more of a fun fact, but I'm sure a seasoned control theorist would find this connection or apporach to MPC and adaptive control exciting. This is in no way a survey or course text, more so a monograph of his research.

All in all, it seems to be a good intro. Coupling this with Algorithms for Decision Making Under Uncertainty, Algorithms for Optimization ([Kochenderfer](https://algorithmsbook.com/optimization/)) was very pleasant, especially  DM.pdf, part II: sequential games.
