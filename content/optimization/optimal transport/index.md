This is a collection of notes on the subject of Optimal Transport, treating [Matthew Thorpe's Introduction to Optimal Transport] as an initial stepping stone into the problem formulation and conceptual frameworks it entails. 

### Why study optimal transport?

In the more immediate context of the [overlearning paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16001), it's nice to have a good grasp of playing with measures, having them converge to another, and the formulations of cost in a continuous setting. Of course, it's not terribly urgent to have a notion of _optimal_ transport to understand the paper at all, though the mentions of KL div, entropy, and converging measures gave me enough of a boost to finally take my time to learn OT, one of the theoretical backbones of modern ML and a topic that plays very well with numerical methods. More to do with diffusion methods, and also couples as an interesting grad topic to start reading on and start exploring different research interests.

I generally like the writing and presentation style of Cambridge profs; it kind of hits a good middle ground of aloofness and serious treatment of the material.
