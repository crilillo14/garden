This is a collection of notes on the subject of Optimal Transport, treating [Matthew Thorpe's Introduction to Optimal Transport] as an initial stepping stone into the problem formulation and conceptual frameworks it entails. 

### Why study optimal transport?

In the more immediate context of the [overlearning paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16001), it's nice to have a good grasp of playing with measures, having them converge to another, and the formulations of cost in a continuous setting. Of course, it's not terribly urgent to have a notion of _optimal_ transport to understand the paper at all, though the mentions of KL div, entropy, and converging measures gave me enough of a boost to finally take my time to learn OT, one of the theoretical backbones of modern ML and a topic that plays very well with numerical methods.

I think a good intro to this is [Thorpe 2018](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/research/cia/files/teaching/Optimal_Transport_Notes.pdf), some lecture notes from a Cambridge course in 2018. I generally like the writing and presentation style of Cambridge profs; it kind of hits a good middle ground of aloofness and serious treatment of the material.
